## 2. Findings (Suspected Bugs, Spec Mismatches, Silent Confounds, or Edge-Case Traps)

**Headline verdict: Zero headline-moving bugs were found.** Across the CLI, TUI, and `run_addition_step_fork` entry points, `step_fork` implements [task.md](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/spec/task.md) and [step_fork.md](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/spec/variants/step_fork.md) faithfully: there is no target or oracle leakage, column-step and target-count parity (`3 * batch_size`) with `cls` and `step_ar` is exact, evaluation rollout is strictly unassisted, and all 29 configuration fields are plumbed end-to-end.

Below are four subtle confounds, spec nuances, and library-API edge cases worth recording before running and interpreting studies.

### [Low] 1. Direct `train()` callers on `AdditionStepForkDataset` are not guarded against `batch_size % 3 != 0`

- **Where**: [dataset.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/dataset.py#L22-L36), [run.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/run.py#L40-L49), and [training.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/yessir/training.py#L319-L325).
- **Trigger**: Calling `yessir.training.train(model, AdditionStepForkDataset(...), max_steps=..., batch_size=64)` directly from a Python script or notebook (mirroring `train(model, AdditionClsDataset(...), batch_size=64)` without multiplying by `AdditionStepForkDataset.BRANCHES_PER_STEP = 3`).
- **Behaviour & impact**:
  - **Standard CLI / TUI / study runs are unaffected**, because [run_addition_step_fork](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/run.py#L47) explicitly passes `train_batch_size=AdditionStepForkDataset.BRANCHES_PER_STEP * config.batch_size`.
  - However, `AdditionStepForkDataset` yields a flat stream of `LabelledExample`s (`[S]`, `[C]`, `[F]` per transition), and `train()` slices `batch_size` items per step without checking `getattr(dataset, "BRANCHES_PER_STEP", 1)`. Passing `batch_size=64` directly to `train()` silently trains on `21.33` column transitions per step instead of `64` and splits every 22nd transition's `([S], [C], [F])` triple across two optimiser updates.

### [Low] 2. `AdditionStepForkDataset` and `iter_problem_steps` do not reject non-default `p_terminal` when `all_steps=True`

- **Where**: [dataset.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/dataset.py#L53-L54), [dataset.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/dataset.py#L99-L128), and [inputs.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/inputs.py#L536-L566) vs [config.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/config.py#L471-L474).
- **Trigger**: Instantiating `AdditionStepForkDataset(all_steps=True, p_terminal=0.8)` in Python or running `.venv-linux/bin/python -m experiments.addition.variants.step_fork.dataset --all-steps --p-terminal 0.8`.
- **Behaviour & impact**:
  - **Training runs via `AdditionStepForkConfig` are protected**: [AdditionStepConfig.__post_init__](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/config.py#L471-L474) raises `ValueError("p_terminal is unused when all_steps=True; leave p_terminal=0.2, ...")`.
  - In `AdditionStepForkDataset` and the dataset inspection CLI (`dataset.py`), only `0.0 <= p_terminal <= 1.0` is checked, so `--all-steps --p-terminal 0.8` silently ignores `--p-terminal 0.8`.

### [Note] 3. Spec phrasing vs three structural differences when comparing `step_fork` against `cls` and `step_ar`

- **Where**: [step_fork.md](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/spec/variants/step_fork.md#L7-L10), [variants-comparison.json](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/studies/addition/variants-comparison.json#L5), [step_fork/inputs.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/inputs.py#L39-L48), [cls/inputs.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/cls/inputs.py#L45-L54), and [heads.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/yessir/heads.py#L21-L83).
- **Details**:
  1. **Sequence length, attention hop count, and `OUTPUT` coordinates relative to `cls`**: [step_fork.md](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/spec/variants/step_fork.md#L8) states that against `cls`, `step_fork` changes *"only the readout mechanism from learned linear heads (`ParallelClassificationHead`) to query-token next-token prediction (`SubspaceLMHead`)"*. Necessary consequences of appending `[S]`/`[C]`/`[F]` also change:
     - Sequence length increases by `+1` token at every step `k`, and historical output coordinates shift from `position_ids = 1 .. k` in `cls` (where `o_{k-1}` is itself the readout token for `k >= 1`) to `position_ids = 0 .. k - 1` in `step_fork` (with `[S]`/`[C]`/`[F]` at `position_id = k`). Under `token_type_nope` and `nope`, `cls` reads from `TokenType.OUTPUT` (`o_{k-1}`) at `k >= 1`, whereas `step_fork` always reads from a trailing `TokenType.SPECIAL` query token.
     - Parameter count drops by `d_model * (1 + 3 * 256) = 393,728` at default `d_model = 512` (`18,978,816` parameters in `step_fork` vs `19,372,544` in `cls`, a `2.07%` difference at 6 layers and a much larger relative share on small models in [minimum-model-size.json](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/studies/addition/minimum-model-size.json#L5)).
     - At step `0`, `SubspaceLMHead`'s parameter-free `RMSNorm(d_model, gain=False)[:, :, :256]` starts with a self-copy bias toward the input query token (`0xF8`, `0xF9`, `0xFA`, giving initial loss `~7.75` nats vs `~5.63` nats in `cls`) and bounds every output logit to `[-sqrt(d_model), +sqrt(d_model)]` (`[-22.63, +22.63]` at `d_model = 512`, which still supports cross-entropy down to `~4e-8` nats). It also requires one extra attention hop to copy `s_{idx}` from `alphabet` into coordinates `0..255` (noted in [test_convergence.py](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/tests/test_convergence.py#L125-L131)).
  2. **3x wall-clock compute (`elapsed_sec`) vs exact optimiser-step parity**: Because `step_fork` runs `3 * batch_size = 192` full sequences per optimiser step (encoding each transition's shared prefix 3 times rather than forking a cached prefix), `step_fork` performs `~3x` the forward/backward FLOPs and activation memory per step compared to `cls` and `step_ar` (`64` sequences/step). As verified below, per-step loss, parameter gradients, `AdamW` updates, and `tokens_seen` are mathematically identical to a shared-prefix fork, so study comparisons across `cls`, `step_fork`, and `step_ar` should compare by `step` or `tokens_seen`, not wall-clock `elapsed_sec` in `train.jsonl` / `eval.jsonl`.

### [Note] 4. Minor distribution and tie-breaking details (non-bugs)

- **Asymmetric operand distribution at the carry-initiating column `i` (`p_chain > 0`)**: In [sample_problem](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/inputs.py#L450-L455), `a_vals[i] = rng.randint(1, base - 1)` followed by `b_vals[i] = rng.randint(base - a_vals[i], base - 1)` always satisfies `v_a + v_b >= B` as specified in [task.md](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/spec/task.md#L79), while weighting smaller `a_vals[i]` more heavily than uniform over the `{v_a + v_b >= B}` triangle (e.g. in base `10`, `(1, 9)` has probability `1/9` vs `1/81` for `(9, 9)`, giving `E[v_a] = 5.0` and `E[v_b] = 7.0` at column `i`).
- **Parallel branch first-error tie-breaking**: When a `step_fork` rollout predicts wrong tokens on both `[S]` (`o_k`) and `[C]` (`c_{k+1}`) at the earliest failing column `k`, [evaluate_step_fork](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/variants/step_fork/evaluation.py#L186-L190) calls [score_predictions](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/evaluation.py#L163-L173) with default `step_order=StepOrder.SUM_FIRST`, deterministically classifying `[S]` (`o_k`) before `[C]` (`c_{k+1}`), matching `cls` and the `(o_k, c_{k+1})` order in [task.md](file:///atelier/code/workshop/yessir/experiments/addition/spec/task.md#L160).
